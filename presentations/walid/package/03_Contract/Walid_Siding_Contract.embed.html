2. Scope of Work

Subject to the confirmations and exclusions in Schedule A, the Contractor shall provide labour, supervision, ordinary hand tools, and installation services for the following exterior work:

  1. Layout and installation of approximately 30 squares (approximately 3,000 square feet) of exterior siding/cladding in a charcoal-and-cedar-tone façade pattern approved by the Customer.
  2. Installation of associated siding trims, starter components, closures, corner treatments, and opening perimeter trims only to the extent expressly identified as Contractor-supplied or Customer-supplied in Schedule A.
  3. Installation of two exterior doors at the agreed locations, including setting, plumbing, levelling, shimming, mechanical fastening, and ordinary perimeter air sealing, subject to the supplied-door and rough-opening conditions in Schedule A.
  4. Routine protection of the Contractor’s active work area, daily housekeeping, and removal of the Contractor’s packaging and installation debris to the designated on-site disposal location.
  5. Coordination with the Customer regarding opening dimensions, sequencing, access, approved layout, and substrate readiness.
  6. Crew fuel/travel allowance for a projected 3-week installation plus 1-week schedule pushback (20 included round trips) between 9A Regency Drive, Dartmouth, NS and the Project site (and back), as priced in Section 3.

The Contractor shall perform the work in a good and workmanlike manner, in accordance with the applicable contract documents and the approved manufacturers’ written installation instructions.

4. Measurement and Quantity Adjustment

Before production installation, the Parties shall jointly review or cause to be reviewed the accessible wall dimensions, rough openings, cladding limits, grade terminations, and approved façade pattern. The supplied concept takeoff estimates approximately 28.0 net installed squares and approximately 30.8 squares with a 10% allowance, which is a reasonableness check only and not a guaranteed final quantity.

If the final measured net scope plus agreed waste/coverage allowance differs from the 30-square pricing basis by more than one square, the contract price shall be adjusted by written change order at $400.00 per added or deleted square, unless the Parties agree to another written rate. Partial squares shall be prorated to the nearest one-tenth of a square.

Under section 13 of the Nova Scotia Builders’ Lien Act, the holdback is generally retained for 60 days after substantial performance, subject to the Act’s conditions, lien notices, remaining work, and release rules.1 On the $14,005.12 pre-HST price, the full contract holdback would be $1,400.51 if the entire price is lienable and no adjustment applies.

Unless an invoice states otherwise, undisputed non-holdback amounts are due within seven calendar days after receipt. The Customer shall notify the Contractor in writing of any disputed amount within three business days, stating the specific reason and the amount withheld. The undisputed balance remains payable when due. Holdback release is subject to the Act and does not waive genuine warranty or deficiency claims.

7. Customer Responsibilities

The Customer shall do the following in time to avoid delay:

  1. Provide lawful and safe access to the Project, work faces, washroom facilities, electrical power, water where reasonably required, and a designated area for material staging and waste.
  2. Provide the latest authorized drawings, permits, approvals, surveys, shop drawings, product data, finish selections, and written coordination information affecting the Contractor’s work.
  3. Ensure the wall substrate, sheathing, air/water barrier, membranes, insulation, furring, blocking, flashings, roof/eave transitions, penetrations, and rough openings are complete, sound, dry, plumb, level, properly integrated, and ready to receive the Contractor’s work, except for items expressly included in Schedule A.
  4. Confirm the legal identity of the owner, constructor/general contractor, Customer, consultant, and party authorized to direct and approve changes.
  5. Arrange uninterrupted access to the work and coordinate other trades so they do not damage, cover, remove, or obstruct the Contractor’s work.
  6. Verify that Customer-supplied materials are correct, undamaged, compatible, code-compliant, complete, delivered on time, and sufficient for the work, including reasonable waste and replacements.
  7. Provide written approval of the façade layout, material colours, transition heights, trim approach, and door locations before installation.

8. Contractor Responsibilities

The Contractor shall:

  1. Supervise and coordinate its own personnel and subcontractors.
  2. Follow applicable occupational-health-and-safety requirements and take every precaution reasonably required within the Contractor’s authority and control. Nova Scotia’s framework assigns duties to owners, constructors, contractors, employers, and workers according to their roles and degree of control; this agreement does not transfer a statutory duty contrary to law.2
  3. Promptly report discovered conditions that materially conflict with the contract documents, safe installation, or manufacturer requirements.
  4. Protect its work until the Customer takes possession or another trade assumes control of the affected area, whichever occurs first.
  5. Maintain records reasonably supporting progress invoices, approved extras, and substantial performance.

9. WCB, Insurance, and Compliance

Before commencing work and on reasonable request, each Party shall provide evidence of the insurance, licences, registrations, and clearances applicable to its role. If required to be registered, the Contractor shall maintain WCB Nova Scotia coverage and provide a current clearance letter. WCB Nova Scotia states that a clearance letter confirms coverage, current payroll reporting, and no outstanding balance, and that hiring parties should check and track contractor clearance.3

The Contractor shall maintain commercial general liability insurance with limits of not less than $2,000,000 per occurrence, or another amount agreed in writing, and automobile insurance as required by law. The Customer shall maintain property and course-of-construction insurance appropriate to the Project and shall remain responsible for existing structures, contents, and work by others.

10. Changes and Extra Work

A change is any addition, deletion, revision, resequencing, acceleration, suspension, changed condition, quantity variation beyond the agreed allowance, or work made necessary by an unready or defective substrate or by others. Except for immediate steps reasonably necessary to protect people or property, the Contractor is not required to perform a change without a written change order describing the scope, price, tax, and schedule effect.

If emergency or time-sensitive direction makes advance pricing impracticable, the Customer may issue a written field directive. The Contractor shall then track labour, materials, equipment, and subcontract costs. Unless another markup is written into the directive, such work shall be priced at documented cost plus 15% overhead and profit. The Customer’s site representative is not authorized to approve extra work unless named in Schedule B.

11. Site Conditions and Substrate

The contract price assumes installation over a substrate meeting the drawings, manufacturer requirements, and ordinary tolerances. The Contractor is not responsible for hidden deterioration, structural movement, moisture trapped behind existing work, missing blocking, incompatible membranes, out-of-plumb framing, inaccurate rough openings, water entry from excluded assemblies, hazardous substances, or prior work by others.

If such a condition is discovered, the Contractor may stop the affected work, protect the area where reasonably possible, and request written direction. Investigation, correction, redesign, drying, testing, demolition, rebuilding, or remobilization is additional work unless expressly included in Schedule A.

12. Materials, Title, and Risk

Responsibility for supplying siding, doors, trims, flashings, fasteners, sealants, membranes, furring, lifts/scaffolding, and disposal bins shall be selected in Schedule A. Product substitutions require written approval.

Customer-supplied products remain at the Customer’s risk. The Contractor is not responsible for delay, colour variation, shortage, damage, incompatibility, or warranty limitations inherent in Customer-supplied materials, except to the extent caused by the Contractor’s negligent handling or installation. Uninstalled Contractor-supplied materials remain the Contractor’s property until paid for, subject to applicable law.

13. Inspection, Deficiencies, and Substantial Performance

The Contractor shall notify the Customer when the work is substantially performed. The Customer shall inspect within three business days and provide one written deficiency list identifying observable contract deficiencies. Minor deficiencies that do not prevent intended use do not delay substantial performance or payment of undisputed amounts.

The Contractor shall correct covered deficiencies within a reasonable time considering weather, access, material availability, and sequencing. Work altered, damaged, removed, or covered by others before inspection is excluded from the Contractor’s correction obligation to the extent the Contractor cannot reasonably verify or access it.

14. Workmanship Warranty

The Contractor warrants its installation workmanship for one year from substantial performance. The warranty is limited to correcting defects in the Contractor’s workmanship. It excludes normal wear, abuse, impact, vandalism, building or substrate movement, condensation, inadequate ventilation, leaks or failures originating outside the Contractor’s scope, defective design, hazardous substances, corrosion or incompatibility not caused by the Contractor, product defects, colour changes, sealant maintenance, extreme weather, and work altered by others.

Manufacturer warranties, if any, are passed through to the Customer to the extent transferable. Warranty service is conditional on the Customer providing reasonable notice and access and having paid all undisputed amounts, without limiting rights that cannot lawfully be excluded.

15. Suspension and Termination

The Contractor may suspend affected work on written notice for non-payment of an undisputed amount, unsafe conditions, lack of required access, unavailable approved materials, unlawful direction, or material breach by the Customer. The Contractor shall be entitled to a reasonable schedule extension and documented demobilization, standby, storage, and remobilization costs caused by the suspension.

Either Party may terminate for material breach if the breaching Party does not cure the breach within seven calendar days after written notice, or sooner where an immediate safety or legal risk cannot reasonably wait. On termination, the Customer shall pay for work performed, materials ordered or committed, demobilization, restocking/cancellation charges, approved changes, and other amounts properly due, less prior payments and subject to applicable holdback law.

16. Indemnity and Limitation

Each Party shall indemnify the other for third-party claims, damage, injury, or loss to the extent caused by that Party’s negligence, wilful misconduct, breach of this agreement, or failure to discharge duties within its control. Neither Party is liable to the other for indirect, special, punitive, or consequential damages, including lost profit or loss of use, except to the extent such a limitation is prohibited by law or the loss is covered by insurance required under this agreement.

The Contractor’s aggregate contractual liability, excluding insured third-party bodily injury/property damage claims, fraud, or wilful misconduct, shall not exceed the contract price actually paid to the Contractor, to the extent enforceable.

17. Dispute Resolution

A Party shall first provide written notice describing the dispute and requested resolution. Senior representatives shall meet within five business days and attempt good-faith resolution. If unresolved, the Parties shall attempt confidential mediation in Nova Scotia before starting court proceedings, except where urgent injunctive relief, lien preservation, limitation periods, or safety requires immediate action.

This agreement is governed by the laws of Nova Scotia and the applicable laws of Canada. The courts of Nova Scotia have jurisdiction, subject to any written arbitration agreement made after the dispute arises.

18. Notices

Notices under this agreement shall be delivered personally, by recognized courier, or by email to the addresses in the party table. An email notice is deemed received when acknowledged by the recipient or, absent acknowledgment, on the next business day if no delivery-failure notice is received. Change orders and site directions may be signed electronically.

19. General Terms

This agreement is the entire agreement concerning the work and replaces prior oral or written discussions about the same scope. A waiver is effective only if written and applies only to the specific matter waived. If a provision is unenforceable, it shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary or severed without invalidating the remainder. Neither Party may assign this agreement without the other’s written consent, except to a corporate affiliate or successor that assumes the obligations and has equivalent financial and operational capacity.

Nothing in this agreement creates a partnership, joint venture, employment relationship, or agency. The Contractor is an independent contractor and controls the means and methods of its work, subject to the contract documents, law, site rules, and coordination obligations.
